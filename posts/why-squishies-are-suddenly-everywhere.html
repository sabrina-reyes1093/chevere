Why Squishies Are Suddenly Everywhere

Jul 28, 2026

From tiny dumplings , cubes and blocks of cheese to impossible-to-find Needoh's and Smushers, the internet’s latest obsession is soft, strange and deeply satisfying.

If your social media feed has recently filled with people squeezing tiny dumplings, flattening cartoon-faced creatures or pressing their fingers into miniature blocks of cheese, you are not alone.

Squishy sensory toys are suddenly everywhere. There are glitter-filled dumplings packaged in tiny bamboo steamers, butter sticks, gummy bears, bread loaves, cheese wedges and funny-faced Smushers that can be stretched, flattened and slowly returned to their original shape.

Squishies are not exactly new. Stress balls, slime and fidget toys have been popular for years. What makes this latest wave feel different is how collectible—and oddly specific—they have become. A basic stress ball is practical. A tiny dumpling with rosy cheeks and its own steamer feels like something you need to find in every color.

They are also perfectly designed for the internet. Close-up squeezing videos, slow-rising textures and satisfying ASMR sounds make the experience entertaining even through a screen. The squish itself becomes the content, encouraging people to compare textures, share restock alerts and search multiple stores for the exact one they want.

Part of the appeal is simple: they give restless hands something to do. You can keep one beside your laptop, squeeze it during a meeting or play with it while watching television. They require no charging, syncing or scrolling. Their only purpose is to be soft, silly and satisfying.

That may explain why adults are just as interested as kids. Like Jellycats, Labubus and miniature collectibles, squishies offer a small, low-stakes form of joy. They are part stress reliever, part desk companion and part treasure hunt.

Will a tiny block of cheese solve your problems? Probably not. But squeezing one for a few seconds might make the day feel slightly better—and apparently, that is more than enough to keep them selling out.
