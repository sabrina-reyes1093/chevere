Spain Wins the 2026 World Cup

Ferran Torres scored the winning goal in extra time as Spain defeated Argentina 1–0 in a dramatic final.

The 2026 World Cup ended with Spain lifting the trophy after defeating defending champion Argentina at New York New Jersey Stadium.

For most of the match, Spain controlled possession and created the better chances. Argentina goalkeeper Emiliano Martínez delivered an outstanding performance, making 12 saves and keeping his team level through 90 minutes.

Argentina’s challenge became even more difficult when Enzo Fernández received a second yellow card late in regulation, forcing the team to play extra time with 10 men.

Spain finally found its breakthrough in the 106th minute. Substitute Ferran Torres reacted quickly to a loose ball inside the penalty area and fired a left-footed shot beyond Martínez. It was Torres’ first goal of the tournament—and the goal that made Spain world champion.

Argentina nearly forced a penalty shootout in the closing minutes, but Giuliano Simeone sent the team’s final opportunity over the crossbar.

A Historic Victory for Spain

The win gave Spain its second men’s World Cup title and its first since 2010. La Roja completed the tournament having conceded only one goal and extended its unbeaten run to 38 matches.

Rodri received the Golden Ball as the tournament’s best player, while Spain became the first nation to hold the men’s and women’s World Cup titles at the same time.

The victory also felt like the beginning of a new generation. Young stars including Lamine Yamal and Nico Williams helped lead a Spanish team built around possession, discipline and depth.

A Difficult Goodbye for Messi

For Lionel Messi, the final brought a painful ending to what is expected to be his last World Cup.

At 39, Messi came within one match of leading Argentina to consecutive titles. Although the storybook ending never arrived, another World Cup final added to an international career that already includes the 2022 World Cup and multiple Copa América trophies.

When the final whistle sounded, Spain celebrated the beginning of a new era while Argentina said goodbye to its reign as champion.

The tournament may have been the largest and loudest World Cup ever staged, but the final came down to one moment: a loose ball, a perfectly timed finish and a substitute becoming Spain’s newest national hero.
