You Need a Hobby That Isn’t Your Phone

Aug 9, 2026

10 things to do simply because you like doing them.

Somewhere along the way, hobbies started feeling weirdly serious.

Everything has to become a side hustle, a personal brand, a wellness practice, or something you can eventually become good at. Even relaxing somehow comes with a performance review.

But a hobby doesn’t need to improve your résumé, make money, or produce anything impressive. It can just be the thing you do because it makes an ordinary Tuesday slightly more interesting.

And maybe that’s exactly why we need them again.

Between work, errands, group chats, and the accidental forty-five minutes spent scrolling without remembering a single thing we saw, there isn’t always much room left for curiosity. So consider this your reminder to reclaim a little of it.

Here are ten hobbies worth trying—not because you should master them, but because having something you genuinely look forward to is kind of the point.

1. Start a Tiny Garden

You do not need a backyard or an elaborate raised-bed situation.

Start with basil on the windowsill. A pot of mint. Cherry tomatoes on the balcony. There is something deeply satisfying about watching something you planted actually become something you can eat.

Bonus: suddenly making pasta feels much more impressive when you casually announce that the basil is from the garden.

2. Take a Ceramics Class

Few things force you to stop looking at your phone quite like having both hands covered in clay.

Ceramics is tactile, slightly messy, surprisingly calming, and you eventually leave with a mug or bowl that is objectively a little crooked but that you will nevertheless treasure forever.

Perfect.

3. Learn to Make One Thing Really Well

Not cooking as an entire hobby. Too broad.

Pick one thing.

Fresh pasta. The perfect tortilla española. Homemade focaccia. Dumplings. Tiramisu. A very good martini.

Spend a few weekends perfecting it until it becomes your thing—the dish people quietly hope you’ll bring whenever you’re invited somewhere.

4. Become a Film Person

Instead of endlessly scrolling through streaming services and eventually rewatching something familiar, give yourself an assignment.

Choose a director and watch their films chronologically. Work through the classics you’ve somehow missed. Pick a country and explore its cinema for a month.

You don't need to become insufferable about aspect ratios afterward. Although we can’t promise you won’t.

5. Start Collecting Something

There is something charmingly old-school about having a collection.

Vintage matchbooks. Postcards from places you visit. Art books. Interesting perfume bottles. Records. Hotel stationery. Cookbooks.

The best collections slowly become little archives of your taste—and, eventually, your life.

6. Take a Dance Class

Salsa. Ballet. Heels. Hip-hop. Bachata. Ballroom.

Being a beginner at something physical is both humbling and weirdly freeing. You’re concentrating too hard on figuring out where your feet are supposed to go to think about much else.

And unlike a workout you have to convince yourself to finish, dancing happens to come with music.

7. Learn a Language Without Making It Homework

Skip the pressure of becoming fluent by a certain date.

Watch movies with subtitles. Listen to music and look up the lyrics. Follow creators who speak the language. Learn enough phrases to order dinner properly on your next trip.

Even understanding a little more of another language changes the way you experience its food, films, books, music, and people.

8. Keep a Commonplace Book

Think of it as a scrapbook for your brain.

Write down lines from books you love. Restaurant names you want to remember. Movie recommendations. Photos. Recipes. Quotes. Exhibition tickets. Random thoughts you would otherwise type into your Notes app and never look at again.

Over time, it becomes a record of everything that caught your attention.

And unlike your camera roll, you might actually revisit it.

9. Try an Analog Craft

Crochet. Embroidery. Collage. Painting. Needlepoint. Flower arranging.

Anything that requires your hands and approximately zero notifications qualifies.

The finished product is almost beside the point. There’s something unusually satisfying about spending an hour making something slowly when so much of the rest of life happens instantly.

10. Become a Tourist in Your Own City

There are probably neighborhoods, museums, restaurants, bookstores, parks, and tiny galleries within an hour of you that you've never visited.

Choose one Saturday a month and go somewhere you've never been.

Order lunch somewhere new. Visit the exhibition you keep saying you'll see. Walk without having anywhere particularly important to be.

Travel is exciting partly because we pay closer attention when we're somewhere unfamiliar. Sometimes you can recreate a little of that feeling without boarding a plane.

---

The point is to be a little unproductive

You don't have to monetize the pottery.

You don't have to post the garden.

You don't have to become fluent, run a film account, sell your paintings, or transform Sunday cooking into content.

Some things are allowed to belong entirely to you.

And in an era when almost every spare moment can be filled by a screen, having something you'd actually rather be doing might be one of the nicest luxuries left.

Pick one. Be mediocre at it. Have fun anyway.
